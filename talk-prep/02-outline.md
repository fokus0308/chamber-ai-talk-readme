# "When Your Customers Ask AI, Do You Come Up?": 45-minute outline

SCATI AI Business Lab · Wed Oct 14 · 8:00–9:00 AM · Buena Vida Estates
Speaking notes, not a script. All evidence lives in `offline-evidence.html` (works without internet).

**The core lesson, in one line:** What can AI find about you, does it get it right, and do you come up when a customer asks for what you do?

**Three things you never say:** "this is why it left you out," "this is your AI ranking," "our work caused this."

---

## 0:00–0:04 · Open: the question
- Ask the room: "Who here has asked ChatGPT or Google's AI to recommend a business?" Hands up.
- "Your customers are doing that right now. Today we check two things: does AI *know* you, and does it *recommend* you? They're different."
- QR code is on screen from minute one: "That's a free checklist and the two exact questions I'm about to use. Nothing to fill out unless you want to."

## 0:04–0:10 · The two tests
- **Test 1, Discovery:** ask the way a customer does, *without* naming the business. "Who should I call for [service] in [city]?" Do you appear?
- **Test 2, Understanding:** ask about the business *by name*. "What is [business] in [city]?" Does it get your services, location and details right?
- Three ground rules, said plainly:
  1. Answers change: by day, by wording, by tool. One answer is a snapshot, not a ranking.
  2. We can see *what* it said and *where it looked*. We can't see *why* it chose.
  3. So we date everything, and we check the sources it read.

## 0:10–0:24 · Under the microscope: Patriot Fire Defense *(only if Patriot said yes; otherwise use the anonymized contrast as the main example)*
- **Understanding first** (slide: the "What is Patriot…" answer, Oct 1). "It knows them: services, phone, family-owned since 2006. Pass."
- Then the twist: "But look. It says BBB lists *two* locations. Their Google listing says Old NASA Blvd, the website says W. NASA Blvd Suite 55." *(Only if verified on Oct 13. Otherwise skip this beat.)*
- **Discovery** (slide: the two kitchen hood answers, Sep 14 and Oct 1). "Kitchen hood suppression in Melbourne is Patriot's bread and butter. Two dates, six different companies named, Patriot in neither."
- **The punchline slide:** same company, same city. Emergency lighting: #1 on both dates. Kitchen hood: not there, both dates.
- Room question: "What would *you* check first?" Take 2–3 answers.
- **What we can point to:** whether there's a page that plainly answers kitchen hood suppression in Melbourne, and whether the address matches everywhere *(from the Oct 13 Chrome checks)*.
- Say the limits out loud: "I can't prove that's why. I can show you it's the gap we can see, and it's cheap to fix."
- **Optional live repeat:** one discovery question in a logged-out window. Whatever happens, use the matching line from `01-examples-and-evidence.md` ("If the live result differs").

## 0:24–0:30 · Contrast: "Known, not recommended"
- Anonymized: "a family-owned Melbourne HVAC company, since 1976, around 1,400 reviews."
- Understanding: it recited their history, and **flagged their conflicting hours, two addresses and duplicate pages on its own.**
- Discovery: **0 of 28** customer questions across two dates.
- Lesson: "Reviews and a long history weren't enough to get named. The AI noticed the mess before any customer did."
- *Backup if needed* (Titusville HVAC, anonymized): "Mentioned isn't recommended. One of its three mentions was a warning."

## 0:30–0:38 · Your turn (phones out)
- Everyone runs **Test 2 on their own business** in a private or incognito window: *"What is [my business] in [my city]?"*
- Then **Test 1** with their main service: *"Who should I call for [service] in [city]? Name three and say why."*
- Score it on the checklist (3 questions): Did it know you? Was anything wrong? Did you come up when it wasn't told your name?
- Walk the room. Ask for one volunteer to read out a surprise. **This is the optional "willing owner" moment**, using the invitation below.

## 0:38–0:42 · Questions
- Likely: "Which AI matters most?" → "Whichever your customers use. Test the two biggest, and test logged out."
- "Can I pay to be recommended?" → "Some platforms show labeled ads near answers. I don't know of any legitimate way to buy a spot inside the recommendation itself, so be wary of anyone selling that. What you control is making what it reads accurate and complete."
- "How long do fixes take?" → "Months, not days. And no one can promise a specific answer."

## 0:42–0:45 · Close
- "Two tests, two minutes, any time. The checklist is on that QR code."
- **The offer, said accurately:** "If you want me to run this for you, the visibility map checks where you show up on Google Maps from different parts of Brevard, and asks an AI assistant with web search a dozen of your customers' questions. I review every map myself before it goes out, and I'll email you when yours is ready."
- No turnaround promise, no claim that it's what ChatGPT or Google will say, no results promise.
- Last line: "Your customers already asked. Go find out what it told them."

## Optional: inviting a willing owner (say aloud, no pressure)
> "Is there an owner here with a storefront or service business who'd be up for a two-minute live check? I'll only look at public information. You'll see everything I see, you can stop me any time, and I'll point out one thing that's working and one easy fix. No one has to. The examples I brought work fine on their own."

**Pick someone if:** they own the business (not an employee), it has a physical location or a clear service area in Brevard, it has a Google listing *and* a website, and they say yes out loud in front of the room.
**Skip if:** they hesitate, the business is brand new or home-based with a hidden address, or it's in a sensitive field (medical, legal, financial). Don't run Test 1 for them unless you're ready to say "it didn't come up today" kindly.
